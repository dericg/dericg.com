02 / CAREER EVIDENCE ENGINEWorking prototype

Turn messy work history into a defensible capability model.

An evidence-first product for answering a difficult career question: what does someone’s actual work prove they can do?

Problem

Résumés make claims, but rarely show how those claims were reached or how strong the underlying evidence is.

Decision

Treat real work as evidence, map each example to capabilities, preserve uncertainty, and make every conclusion inspectable.

Result

The model can distinguish demonstrated strength, partial evidence, and unknowns without forcing every capability into a flattering answer.

One useful idea

Separate evidence from interpretation.

A principle I’m trying to apply both to life and to technical work.

When something happens, I tend to experience two things almost simultaneously: the event itself and my explanation of what the event means. Those aren’t the same thing.

What do I actually know?
What am I interpreting?
What evidence would change my interpretation?

That distinction matters in production systems too: start with observed behavior, gather evidence, isolate dependencies, test a hypothesis, and make the smallest defensible change.

Why this matters

Good judgment is often less about having a fast answer and more about keeping the story separate from the evidence long enough to see what is actually happening.

How I work

Principles that shape the work.

The point is not to collect quotes. It’s to identify ideas strong enough to change how I behave.

01

Face reality early.

Bad news gets more expensive with time. Surface state and failure while there are still options.

02

Separate evidence from interpretation.

Diagnose before changing things. Observed behavior and the story about it are different artifacts.

03

Turn recurring problems into systems.

If the same manual intervention keeps appearing, I start asking whether the workflow itself is the problem.

What I’m exploring now

How should AI participate in systems people need to trust?

I’m interested in AI as a reasoning and synthesis layer—especially when evidence, reviewability, and auditability matter.

Useful AI should make the system more legible, not less.

The current question is where AI can reduce cognitive load without hiding the evidence, removing human judgment from consequential steps, or making system behavior impossible to reconstruct later.
